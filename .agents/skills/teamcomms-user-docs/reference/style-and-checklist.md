# Style and pre-publish checklist

## Audience and tone

Write for employees, organization owners and admins, team members, external collaborators, and first-time users. Assume basic computer and mobile-app skills, but no software development knowledge.

- Use plain English and short sentences.
- Prefer familiar words. Explain a product term on first use.
- Address the reader as "you". Use active voice.
- Be direct and helpful. Do not sound condescending.
- Avoid marketing language, exaggerated claims, and long introductions.
- Do not use em dashes. Use periods, commas, or parentheses.
- If a detail is unclear, ask. Do not fill the gap with a guess.

## Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| User | A person with a Teamcomms account |
| Organization | A workspace for a company or group |
| Organization member | A user who belongs to an organization |
| Team | A group within an organization |
| Team member | A user who belongs to a particular team |
| Owner | The person responsible for an organization |
| Administrator | A user with management permissions |
| Member | A user with standard permissions |
| Invitation | A request to join an organization or a team |
| Message | A post in a team conversation |

Use these terms consistently. Do not use them interchangeably. Do not use developer terms such as API, database, schema, endpoint, tenant, ORM, or middleware in end-user guides.

## Screenshots

When screenshots are available:

- Use them to clarify navigation and unfamiliar interface elements.
- Keep them current with the app.
- Add a short caption or alternative text.
- Crop unrelated interface details.
- Hide private messages, email addresses, and confidential organization information.
- Do not create fake screenshots or present a mockup as the real app.

When screenshots are unavailable, use clear text instructions and do not claim to show the interface.

## FAQ answers

Write FAQs from real user questions, such as:

- Can I belong to more than one organization?
- Can I join more than one team?
- Does joining an organization give me access to every team?
- Who can invite me to a team?
- What happens to my access when I leave an organization?
- Why can I no longer see a team?

Answer the question directly in the first sentence, then add a short explanation or a link to a detailed guide.

## Formatting

- Use a descriptive, task-oriented title.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- Use headings to divide a long procedure.
- Prefer lists over dense paragraphs when there are several options.
- Use tables to compare roles or permissions.
- Link related articles.
- Avoid extra headings in a short article.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] The audience is non-technical.
- [ ] The article solves one clear user problem.
- [ ] The title describes the user's goal.
- [ ] Every instruction matches the current app.
- [ ] Required roles and permissions are accurate.
- [ ] Organization, team, and member terms are used correctly.
- [ ] Access and membership changes are explained.
- [ ] No unimplemented feature is presented as available.
- [ ] The article is short, readable, and easy to follow.
- [ ] Related links and screenshots are valid.
- [ ] No private or confidential information is exposed.
